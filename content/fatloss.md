# Fat Loss

In essence, fat loss is very simple. However, this does not mean it is easy. To lose weight, one must be in a calorie deficit (expend more energy than they consume). However, there are many more things that contribute to successful fat loss, which we will look at deeper below.

> **Weight Loss vs. Fat Loss:** These terms are often thought of as interchangeable, but they are not. We use weight as a measure because it is easy and accessible, but it’s important to note that you do not necessarily have to lose weight to lose fat. Muscle weighs more than fat, so if you are putting on muscle, you might not see the number on that scale dropping as fast as you’d like. That is okay. We use our weight as a tool and a guide, but not the be-all and end-all.
> 

## Creating a Calorie Deficit

A calorie deficit is a necessary component of weight loss. We will not lose weight if we do not use more energy than we take in through our diet. This forces the body to use its stored energy.

### Estimating Your Daily Calorie Needs

Your daily calorie expenditure is made up of:

- **BMR** (Basal Metabolic Rate)
- **NEAT** (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis, e.g., fidgeting, household chores)
- **TEF** (Thermic Effect of Food - calories burned digesting food)
- **Exercise**

Your BMR is the amount of calories your body burns at rest. It is heavily influenced by your genetics and body composition, so estimating your exact daily energy needs can be difficult. Online calorie calculators can provide a good starting estimate.

### Tracking Your Weight Safely

From your initial baseline estimate, track your weight regularly to see how it fluctuates with the changes you make:

- **Consistency is key:** Always try to weigh yourself in the morning before eating or drinking.
- **Look at the trend:** Your weight will naturally fluctuate a lot day-to-day. For this reason, regular weigh-ins are recommended over just once a week, as it gives you a better picture of the overall trend.
- **Menstrual cycle considerations:** Women should be aware of how their menstrual cycle affects their weight. Consider comparing week 1 of your cycle to week 1 of your previous cycle, rather than looking strictly week-to-week.
- **The ideal rate of loss:** Aim to lose weight at a rate of **0.5–1kg per week**. Losing more than 1kg per week is likely too fast (unless you have a lot of weight to lose). If you drop weight too quickly, you may begin to lose muscle mass, and your body may compensate for this extreme rate of loss, making it harder to lose fat going forward. Slow, sustainable, consistent progress is the key.

## Designing Your Diet

Because exercise only contributes a small proportion of our total daily energy expenditure, our main focus should be on improving our diet, using exercise as a supplement to our healthy eating habits.

- **Start easy:** When starting to diet, do not go 100% from the start. There will come a point where you need to give more effort, and if you are already maxed out, progress will stall. This is why a lot of people fail. While motivation is high, they try to do everything perfectly, which is unsustainable. Being good most of the time beats being perfect sometimes.
- **The "least work" approach:** Start by identifying your main unhealthy habits and make small changes to those. Switch to diet sodas, limit takeaways to once a week, add a few more veggies to your plate, or go to the gym once a week. Look to do the least amount of restrictive work possible that still moves you toward your goal. Gradually, over time, the effort you put in will naturally increase, but it won’t feel as difficult because you did it in small, sustainable steps.
- **Don't demonize foods:** Dieting should not feel like absolute hell. Find ways to make your favorite foods healthier. Air-fried fries are healthy, super filling, and a great option. Potatoes, in general, are one of the best weight loss foods because they are versatile, enjoyable, easy to cook, and very satiating.

## Exercise and BMR

Exercise is only responsible for a small portion of your daily calorie expenditure, while your BMR makes up by far the biggest portion. Luckily, by lifting weights and building more muscle, you can increase your BMR. Muscle requires more energy to be built and maintained. Combining strength training with a healthy diet is the best way to lose weight and keep it off in the long run.

## Debunking Fad Diets

Online fitness spaces are filled with articles promoting "perfect diets" or gimmicks claiming Hollywood models lose belly fat by eating only air-fried mango. This is complete nonsense. There is no perfect diet, there are no magical foods that make you lose weight, and there is no one-size-fits-all approach.

Every successful diet relies on a few basic core principles:

1. **Create a Calorie Deficit**
2. **Prioritize Protein**
3. **Start Slow**
4. **Weight Train**

The rest of your diet should be built entirely around what fits you and your unique lifestyle.

### A Look at Popular "Weight-Loss" Diets

- **Keto / Atkins / Low Carb:** This is one of the most popular approaches, yet there is nothing magical about it. People lose weight on it simply because eliminating an entire food group naturally reduces their calorie intake. If you find it works for you, go ahead; however, for most people, this diet proves too restrictive and unenjoyable over time.
- **Vegan / Carnivore:** These follow the same mechanism as low-carb diets. By severely eliminating a major food group, your calories will naturally be a lot lower. A vegan diet tends to be lower in calories, allowing you to eat a larger volume of food, but you must be careful to get enough protein. A carnivore diet is high in protein and very satiating, but you risk missing out on vital micronutrients from fruits and vegetables. A balanced diet is vastly healthier and more sustainable in the long run.
- **Yo-Yo Diets (e.g., Juice Cleanses):** These are classic Hollywood fad diets. You will lose weight extremely fast to begin with because you are eating practically nothing. However, your body will be forced to adapt to this extreme deficit by downregulating your daily energy expenditure. Once your metabolism adjusts to running on extremely low calories, returning to your normal way of eating causes you to put all the lost weight back on - and in most cases, gain even more.
- **Intermittent Fasting:** This has gained massive popularity over the last decade, but there is absolutely nothing magical about it. By limiting your eating window to 8 or 10 hours, you simply tend to eat less than if you were eating across a typical 16-hour waking period. If it fits your schedule, use it. If it doesn't, skip it - you aren't missing out on any special metabolic benefits.
