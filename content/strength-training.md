# Strength Training and Muscle Mass: The Importance of Muscle Mass

Studies have shown a significant correlation between muscle mass, strength, and mortality. Whether this is a direct cause or a knock-on effect (e.g., the inability to stay active) is less clear. However, the exact mechanism is less important than the outcome.

It is undeniable that improved muscle mass and strength allow you to stay physically active as you age, reducing overall mortality risk and improving quality of life. Strength training is also key for preventing osteoporosis (loss of bone density), and increased muscle mass aids in weight management by increasing your BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate—the amount of calories your body burns each day at rest).

## Who Should Be Strength Training?

**Everyone!** * **Age-related decline:** Muscle loss begins as early as thirty. Muscle mass decreases by approximately 3–8% per decade after the age of 30.

- **The risk of inaction:** As we age, this rate of muscle loss speeds up. If unaddressed, it can lead to an inability to perform simple daily tasks, a loss of independence, fragility, falls, and the need for full-time care.
- **It's never too late:** Thankfully, this process can easily be slowed by strength training. If you are not yet affected by age-related muscle loss, focus on building a solid base now. If you are older, you can still improve your strength, muscle function, and reduce further decline.
- **Cardiovascular support:** Preserving muscle mass ensures you remain physically capable of training your cardiovascular system, which is one of the biggest indicators of lifespan.

## How to Strength Train

Online information regarding exercise selection, training splits, and "must-do" movements can make weight training seem complicated. In reality, it is very simple. You only need to consider five core principles: **Frequency, Intensity, Progressive Overload, Technique, and Rest.**

### 1. Frequency

- Aim to train each muscle group **twice a week**.
- This can easily be accomplished with two full-body days.
- Sessions do not have to be long; two 45–60 minute workouts focusing on compound movements (exercises involving multiple muscle groups) is plenty.

### 2. Intensity

- Intensity is what people get wrong most often. Workouts need to be challenging; you must push close to the point of **failure** (the point where you could not possibly do another repetition).
- People often overestimate how hard they are working (e.g., stopping at 10 reps when they could have done 15). Once you are confident with your technique, safely take a set to absolute failure every few sessions so you know where your limit actually is.
- Train within **3 reps of failure** on every set. Building and maintaining muscle requires significant energy; if you do not train hard, you will not stimulate the body to adapt and grow stronger.

### 3. Progressive Overload

- This means trying to train a little bit harder each session by adding **reps, sets, or weight**.
- Progressive overload ensures your intensity remains high. If your training is effective, what felt hard at the start of a training block will no longer be hard enough to stimulate growth.

### 4. Technique

- Proper technique helps you avoid injury and ensures you are targeting the correct muscles. Ask a personal trainer to check your form or utilize instructional videos online.
- **Tempo (Speed):** Focus on contracting fast (the lifting phase) and controlling the lengthening phase (the lowering phase), taking at least one full second. For a squat, this means controlling the way down and standing back up powerfully.
- *Note: A hyper-slow lowering phase does not increase muscle mass any more than a standard, controlled lowering phase.*
- **Rep Ranges & Sets:** The specific number of repetitions doesn't matter as long as you push close to failure. The general recommendation is **8–12 reps for 2–3 sets** per exercise.

### 5. Rest

- Rest at least **one minute between sets**. Listen to your body and take as much time as you need to recover your strength for the next set.
- Take at least **one full day of rest** before training the same muscle group again. During recovery, prioritize sleep and a protein-rich diet.
- *Note: Muscle soreness (DOMS) is normal when you first start, but it will decrease over time. Muscle damage and pain do not drive growth; a workout does not need to make you sore to be effective.*

## How to Make Strength Training Enjoyable

Enjoyment is the key to consistency. If weight lifting feels repetitive, use these strategies to make it more engaging:

- **Listen to music:** Keep your energy up with your favorite playlists.
- **Track your progress:** Documenting your stats boosts motivation and ensures you are achieving progressive overload. Try using a dedicated tracking app like *RepCount*.
- **The "Gym-Only" entertainment rule:** Save a specific podcast or video show exclusively for the gym. You'll find yourself looking forward to your workout just to catch the next episode.
- **Find a gym buddy:** Training with a partner adds an element of fun and keeps you both accountable.
- **Create a positive routine loop:** Pair the gym with something you love. For example, if you love reading at a local coffee shop, go there immediately after your workout as a reward.
