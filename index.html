Aging

8%

Muscle mass lost per decade after thirty

After 30, age-related muscle loss can progress decade by decade and compounds into frailty. Progressive resistance training is one of the most reliable ways to slow this decline and protect function.

Metabolic

Higher all-cause mortality from insulin resistance

In a U.S. nondiabetic cohort, higher insulin resistance (HOMA‑IR) was associated with higher all-cause mortality; the top quartile showed roughly ~1.6× (up to ~2.5×) higher risk versus the lowest quartile depending on subgroup.

Better sleep quality from resistance training

A pilot resistance-training intervention in older adults reported a ~38% improvement in subjective sleep quality (PSQI), alongside meaningful strength gains—suggesting strength work can directly improve sleep.

Extra years of life from higher midlife fitness

In a 46-year follow-up, men with higher midlife cardiorespiratory fitness were estimated to live ~4.9–6.4 years longer than those below normal fitness, reinforcing CRF as a powerful longevity lever.
